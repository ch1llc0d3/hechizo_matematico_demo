How the spell works: each profile becomes a vector in a 3-dimensional latent space (tech, coffee, asunción — the same feature map as the Python spell). The engine computes sim = A·B / (|A|·|B|), the angle θ = acos(sim), and the Love Probability as sim^1.3 × 100. Above 80% the vectors are already staring at each other in latent space… 👀

    The Popularity Game, in your browser 📈

    Imagine a tiny dating app with 50 singles. Every time two of them cross paths, their popularity score goes up or down — the same trick chess clubs use to rank players, and the same idea real matchmaking apps use behind the scenes. Press start and watch popularity happen. No math degree required.

      How the spell works (the friendly version): everyone starts with the same popularity score (1500). When two singles meet, the score moves like a chess rating: a good date lifts both of them, while a bad date stings — and it stings the less popular one much more (the popular one shrugs off 70% of it). Big surprises move scores the most: if an underdog wins over a superstar, the underdog's score rockets. And there's a matchmaker in the background making sure the popular kids don't hog all the attention — it deliberately mixes things up so everyone gets a chance to meet. The fairness meter shows how evenly the attention is spread: full bar = everyone's getting some love. For the curious, the exact recipe is the classic Elo formula E = 1 / (1 + 10^((eloB − eloA)/400)) with an upset bonus K = 32 · (1 + gap/400)^1.4 (capped at 128) and a matchmaker that blends merit-based and random pairing (weight 0.6).

      The Swipe Lab, in your browser 🔥

      How does a dating app decide who you see? Everyone carries a hidden desirability score. You swipe; the app does the math. Like someone out of your league and you'll probably be left on read — but if they like you back, the underdog bonus rockets your score. Drag the card or use the buttons.

          How the spell works: every profile has an Elo score — the app's estimate of how desirable they are. When you like someone, the app computes your win probability E = 1 / (1 + 10^((theirElo − yourElo)/400)): that's the chance they swipe back. Match → both scores rise, but beating someone above your league pays far more (upset bonus K = 32 · (1 + gap/400)^1.4). Rejected → your score drops, and theirs climbs a little, because being wanted (and choosing) is itself a signal. Passing withholds attention, which slowly deflates the passed-on profile. This is the same rating loop as elo_ranking_sim.py — except here you are one of the nodes.
